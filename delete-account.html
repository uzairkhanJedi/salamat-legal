Outing — Delete Your Account

Last updated: 3 September 2026

You can delete your Outing account and the data attached to it at any time. You do not need to have the app installed to make the request. This page explains how to ask, exactly what gets deleted, and what is kept.

Outing is operated by Uzair Khan. Contact: hello@outingapp.io

How to request deletion

Email hello@outingapp.io from the email address on your account, with the subject “Delete my Outing account”. That is all we need — the address you send from is what identifies you.

If you signed in with Apple or Google, use the address that sign-in gave us; it is shown in the app under You → Account. Adding your display name helps us find you faster, but it is not required.

Because deletion is permanent, we act on the address you write from rather than on details someone else could know — the people in your outings already know your name. If you cannot send from that address — you have lost access to the mailbox, or you used Sign in with Apple’s “Hide My Email” — write to us anyway and say so, and we will agree another way to confirm it is you before anything is deleted. If we cannot match your request to an account, or cannot satisfy ourselves that it is yours, we will write back and ask. We will not delete an account on a guess.

We will action your request within 30 days of receiving it, and email you to confirm once it is done. In practice it is usually the same week. There is no charge, and you do not have to give a reason.

Deleting from inside the app

Open You → Account → Delete account and confirm twice. It takes effect immediately, and it removes everything listed under what gets deleted — including your profile photo and your sign-in methods. You do not need to email us as well. This is the fastest route, and it needs no identity check because you are already signed in.

A past bug, recorded here because it was ours. In every version released before 19 July 2026, this button did not actually delete anything — it signed you out and reported success while your profile, photo and location history stayed on our server. It was fixed that day and has worked since. We are sorry; it was wrong for as long as the button existed. If you deleted your account before that date, it did not take effect — please use the email route above and we will finish it properly.

What gets deleted

When we action your request, the following are permanently deleted from our server — not hidden or deactivated, but erased. Please also read what is kept below, which is short and honest about the exceptions.

  • Your profile — email address, display name, language preference, and push notification token.
  • Your location history — every stored location reading, in full.
  • Your current location — the position shown on an outing's map, and the battery level shared alongside it.
  • Your outing memberships — you are removed from every outing you are in. The other members stop seeing you on the map immediately.
  • Your messages and alerts — the outing chat messages you sent, the alerts you exchanged with a leader in both directions, and any announcements you sent.
  • Your invites and requests — invites you sent or received, and any join requests, along with the people connections shown on your People tab.
  • Your profile photo — every image file you uploaded is removed from storage, not just the one currently shown.
  • Your sign-in methods — your account itself, including any passkey and any Sign in with Apple or Google link.
If you lead an outing, we hand it over before deleting you. Any outing you lead that is live or still to come is passed to another member — a co-broadcaster if there is one, otherwise the longest-standing member — so nobody is left in an outing that cannot be ended or extended. If you are its only member, the outing simply ends.

What is kept, and for how long

We want to be straight with you about the small amount that does not disappear:

  • Crash diagnostics — up to 90 days. If the app crashed while you were using it, the crash report is stored by our error-reporting provider (Sentry) and is tagged with your account's random internal ID. These reports contain your app version, device model, and operating system — never your name, email address, photo, or location. They age out automatically within 90 days. Once your account is deleted, the ID they carry no longer points to anything.
  • Product-analytics events — kept. A small set of events recording that you signed in, that you created or joined an outing, whether you granted location permission, and whether your phone managed to send a background update. They are stored by our analytics provider (PostHog, in the European Union) and tagged with the same random internal ID. They contain no location of any kind — not coordinates, not a place name, not a city — and no name, email address or photo. Once your account is deleted, the ID they carry no longer points to anything.
  • Things you created for an outing — kept, but no longer linked to you. If you set a meeting point, or created the outing itself, that content stays so the other members do not lose it. Your name is unlinked from it, so it is no longer attributable to you. Nothing here contains your location or your contact details.
  • Announcements a leader sent you — kept. These belong to that leader's account rather than yours, so deleting your account does not remove them. If a leader wrote your name into the text of an announcement, that text stays until they delete it or delete their own account.
We keep no ongoing backups. When your data is deleted it is gone from our server, not moved to an archive we could restore it from. Nothing we keep after your request can be used to identify you, contact you, or work out where you have been.

One temporary exception: on August 21, 2026 we moved our server from Google Cloud (Doha) to Supabase (Montréal, Canada). The old server and its one-time migration copy are kept for roughly one week after the move as a safety net, then permanently destroyed. If you delete your account during that week, your data is removed from the live server immediately, but a copy may persist in the migration snapshot until it is destroyed (by mid-September 2026 at the latest).

If you just want to stop sharing your location

Deleting your account is permanent and cannot be undone — you would need a new invite code to rejoin. If you only want to stop sharing your position, you can turn Location sharing off in Settings, or leave your group, and keep your account. Your location history is deleted automatically after 7 days either way.
